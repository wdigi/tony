Phase 3 · Day 2

A method for finding faults

Find any fault with the same calm steps, instead of guessing.

📝Update progress on the message board below ↓

The method

  1. Reproduce: see the problem yourself. What exactly happens, when, and since when?
  2. What changed? Most faults follow a change: an update, a move, a power cut, a new cable.
  3. Start simple: power, cables, network, then software. Check the easy things first.
  4. Split in half: is it before or after this point? Network or device? Keep halving.
  5. Change one thing at a time and test after each change.
  6. Write down every step and result while you work, not after.

Practice

Your coach sets up three broken systems at once (screen, LED, service). Use the method on each and keep a timed log.

A worked example: “the window lights are off”

The known-good swap

When you can't tell which part is bad, swap one part for one you know works: a spare card, a cable from a working screen, a different supply. If the fault moves with the part, you've found it. DiGiKits keeps a shelf of known-good spares for this reason (Day 7).

Traps that waste hours

  • "It must be the last thing I touched." Maybe, but test it; don't assume.
  • Seeing only what fits your idea. If one result doesn't fit, your idea is wrong, not the result.
  • Changing three things at once. It works now, but you don't know why, so you can't write the note or prevent it.
  • No time limit. Stuck for 30 minutes on a Severity 1? Tell your coach (Day 8). Fresh eyes are fast.

Done when

  • All three faults found, with a clear log of how
  • You can explain why "change one thing at a time" matters
  • You ranked a list of suspects by likely and easy
  • You used a known-good swap to prove a faulty part
  • Your timed log for each fault reads like the worked example

Support drill

Two faults at once on the same Pi. The method still works; prove it.

More cases

Try each one before you open the answer.

The fault disappears every time you arrive on site.

Find what's different when you're there: time of day, lights on, the door open, the strip at a different brightness. Leave a log running and ask staff to note the time when it happens.

Swapping the SD card fixed the screen. Done?

Not yet: check the old card (is it worn out? corrupted?) so you can write the cause and tell whether other screens with the same age cards are at risk.

You've tried for 40 minutes on a Severity 1 and you're going in circles.

Stop and escalate now (Day 8). Bring your log: it lets your coach help in two minutes instead of twenty.

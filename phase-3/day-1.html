Phase 3 · Day 1

What support is

How a support case flows, from "it's broken" to "fixed and written down".

📝Update progress on the message board below ↓

Why it matters

A clear process means nothing is forgotten, the customer always knows what's happening, and every fix makes the next one faster.

A ticket holds

Ticket #     Customer / site     Opened (date, time)
Problem:     in the customer's words
Severity:    1 down / 2 broken but working around it / 3 question
Steps taken: what you did, in order, with times
Status:      open / waiting on customer / fixed / closed
Next update: when the customer hears from you next

Practice

Your coach gives you five sample problems. Open a ticket for each, set its severity and write the first reply.

Where cases come from

Triage: the first five minutes

Before you fix anything, find out enough to set the severity and plan. Ask (or check):

  • What exactly is wrong? What do they see or hear?
  • Where: which site, which device?
  • Since when, and what changed around then?
  • How many are affected: one screen or all of them?
  • Is there a work-around for now?

Done when

  • You can draw the case flow from memory
  • Five sample tickets have the right severity
  • You can list the five triage questions
  • You know the first-reply time for each severity
  • Your sample tickets have times in Steps taken and a Next update

Practice cases

Try each one before you open the answer.

“The LED colours in the window look a bit off since yesterday.” Severity?

3, or 2 if it's a window display during opening hours. It works, it's not down. Ask: which colours, all of it or one part, did anything change yesterday?

Monitoring: the lab test station stopped reporting at 02:00. No customer has noticed. Severity?

Depends on the lab's schedule: if customer tests run on it this morning, 1. Open the ticket yourself now; you'll probably fix it before anyone notices.

A customer emails a long story with three different problems.

One ticket per problem, each with its own severity. Reply once, listing all three and what happens next for each.

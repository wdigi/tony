Phase 3 · Day 8

Escalation and incidents

Know when a problem is bigger than you, and what to do in the first ten minutes.

📝Update progress on the message board below ↓

Escalate when

  • A customer's system is fully down during business hours and you can't fix it in 30 minutes.
  • Anything involving safety, mains power, smoke or heat.
  • Possible security problem: unknown logins, changed passwords, strange traffic.
  • Data might be lost, or you're about to do something you can't undo.

First ten minutes of an incident

  1. Make it safe (power off if there's any heat or smell).
  2. Tell your coach and the customer: what's affected, what you know, when the next update comes.
  3. Start a timeline log right away.
  4. Restore service first (a spare unit, a restart, a backup), find the root cause after.

After an incident

Write a short review: what happened, timeline, cause, what fixed it, what changes so it's caught faster next time. No blame.

Practice

Your coach runs a surprise incident drill. Handle it, then write the review.

Who does what in an incident

The status update

Subject: [Update 2] Lobby screens down – client shop

What's affected: both lobby screens are black. Window lights are fine.
What we know: the screens lost their network at 10:12. The store's internet is up.
What we're doing: a technician is on the way with spare units, arriving 11:15.
Next update: 11:30, or sooner if anything changes.

Same four lines every time: affected, known, doing, next update. Numbered, so nobody reads an old one by mistake.

If it might be a break-in

  • Don't delete or "clean up" anything. Logs are evidence.
  • Disconnect it from the network (unplug the network cable or turn off its Wi-Fi), unless heat or smoke means power off first.
  • Tell your coach at once, by phone if urgent. Not on the public message board.
  • Change passwords from a different, safe device, as your coach directs.

A review, filled in

What happened: both lobby screens went dark for 63 minutes during opening hours.
Cause: the small network switch behind the screens failed; both screens hang off it.
What fixed it: spare screens plugged into the store's main switch; the failed switch replaced next day.
What went well: alert in 2 minutes; customer updated every 30 minutes.
What changes: a spare switch goes on the shelf; Kuma now checks the switch too, so we know it's the switch, not the screens.

No names, no blame: the question is always "what lets this happen?", never "who did it?"

Done when

  • Drill handled with updates on time
  • Review written and approved
  • You can name the three incident jobs
  • Your drill status updates use the four lines and are numbered
  • You can list the four steps for a possible break-in

More cases

Try each one before you open the answer.

You find a login on a client Pi from an account nobody recognises.

Possible break-in: don't delete anything, disconnect it from the network, call your coach now. Note the time and what you saw.

Your own change caused the outage.

Same steps as any incident: restore service, tell the customer what happened plainly, then a blameless review. What in the process let one change take it down?

The customer asks for an update every 5 minutes.

Agree a rhythm you can keep (e.g. every 30 minutes, or right away if anything changes) and stick to it exactly. Reliable updates calm people more than frequent ones.

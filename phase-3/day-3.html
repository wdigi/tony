Phase 3 · Day 3

Talking to customers

Write replies that calm people down and tell them exactly what happens next.

📝Update progress on the message board below ↓

Rules

  • Answer within the agreed time, even if only to say "I'm on it".
  • Plain words. No jargon unless the customer uses it first.
  • Say what you found, what you did and what happens next, with a time.
  • Never blame the customer. Never guess out loud.
  • Never promise "perfect" or "it won't happen again". Promise to catch problems fast, say so plainly and fix them quickly.

A reply that works

Hi Sam,
Thanks for letting us know the lobby screen is dark.
I checked it remotely: the screen is on, but the TV switched to the wrong input
after this morning's power cut. I switched it back and the page is showing again.
I've added a check so it switches back on its own next time.
I'll look at it again tomorrow at 9:00 and confirm it's still fine.

Practice

Write replies for your Day 1 tickets: one first reply, one "still working on it", one "fixed".

On the phone

Guiding someone who isn't technical

  • One instruction at a time, and wait: "Can you see a small black box with a green light behind the TV?"
  • Describe what they'll see: "When you press it, the light should blink."
  • Ask what they see, not what they think: "What does the screen show right now?", not "Is it connected?"
  • Never make them feel silly. "That's a common one" helps.

Words that help, words that hurt

Instead ofSay
You must have unplugged it.It looks like the strip lost power. Could you check the plug behind the counter?
That's not possible.That's unusual. Let me look into it.
The DHCP lease expired and…The screen lost its network connection after the power cut.
I don't know.I'll find out and get back to you by 2 p.m.
It won't happen again.I've added a check that alerts us if it happens again.

When the customer is upset

Saying no, kindly

Sometimes the answer is no: it's not our equipment, or it needs a licensed electrician. Say what you can do: "The printer isn't something we support, but their maker's help line is on the label. The screen is ours, and that's fixed."

Done when

  • Your coach approves your replies without changes
  • You can run a practice phone call with the five-part shape
  • You rewrote three jargon sentences in plain words
  • Your reply to the angry customer follows listen, acknowledge, act, follow up

Support drill

Reply to an angry message from a customer whose screen was down during an event.

More cases

Try each one before you open the answer.

Customer: “This is the third time this month! Is this even worth paying for?”

Acknowledge first: “I understand, three times is too many, and I'm sorry.” Then facts and action: what you found, what you're changing so it's caught early, and when you'll report back. Tell your coach: three repeats is a pattern.

You promised an update by 14:00 and you have no news.

Send it anyway at 14:00: “Still working on it. I've ruled out X and Y; next I'm checking Z. Next update by 16:00.”

The customer asks something you're not sure about.

Don't guess out loud. “Good question, let me check and get back to you by …” Then really check.

Co-op: Junior Developer

Your home page as a DiGiKits Junior Developer (Co-op). This is a real job: you'll build a real product, the DiGiKits 3D Concept App, help prepare it for customers, and learn enough of the hardware side to talk easily with the hardware team. Start here, then follow the path below.

Your job at a glance

  • When: part-time now, alongside your studies, then a summer role in 2027.
  • Where: at the office, with hybrid work possible.
  • Who you report to: your mentor, an experienced developer who guides your work day to day, reviews your code and writes your reviews. The founder sets the product direction.
  • Your work belongs to the company: everything you build for DiGiKits belongs to DiGiKits Inc., and you sign an IP (intellectual property) agreement before you start.
  • Timesheets from day one: log your hours and what you worked on every day. The company needs these records.

Start dates and the job agreement itself are kept privately, not on this page.

Your project: the 3D Concept App

The 3D Concept App helps building-supply customers see a design idea in 3D before they buy: they describe it, add photos, and the app builds a 3D concept they can look at and change. You'll work on four things:

You'll learnHow
Web and mobile app development on a real productBuilding features in a React + TypeScript web app that installs on a phone like an app (a PWA), and later ships to the app stores with Capacitor from the same code
Applied AI, computer vision and 3D geometryTesting photo-to-3D methods and measuring how accurate they are
Professional practiceGit, code review, documentation and working with customers

The app's inner workings, the customers and the pilot details are explained by your coach when you start; they're internal, so they're not on this page.

Side project: an internal dashboard

Alongside your main job, you'll have a smaller side project: an internal dashboard, a web page that shows live status and numbers from one part of the business at a glance. It uses what you learn in the deploy and pipeline sessions (a small server, Docker, the pipeline) and gives you a second, simpler codebase you own end to end. It follows the same company stack as every DiGiKits tool: started from the project template, a React web page, a small Python FastAPI server, Docker Compose and Caddy on the company's DigitalOcean server.

How you work day to day

  • Small tasks. Every piece of work starts as a small GitHub issue.
  • Branch, pull request, review. You never push straight to main; the pipeline checks your work and a person reviews it.
  • AI coding tools every day. Claude drafts with you; you test, decide what goes in, and own the result (Lesson 8).
  • Write it down. Every fix and decision ends up in a README, a guide or the knowledge base.
  • Weekly check-in with your mentor and a written task plan. Each week: what you did, what's next, what's blocking you.
  • Reviews. A mid-point and an end-of-term review with your mentor (and your school's co-op forms, if the job runs as a co-op term).
  • Timesheet. Hours and tasks logged at the end of every working day.

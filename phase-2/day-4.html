Phase 2 · Day 4

The Big Screen, part 2: control

Turn the TV on and off from the Pi, and switch what it shows with a physical button.

Why it matters

Clients want screens to switch on at opening time and off at night, and staff want one button, not a keyboard.

TV control over HDMI (CEC)

sudo apt install cec-utils
echo 'scan' | cec-client -s -d 1        # what is connected?
echo 'standby 0' | cec-client -s -d 1   # TV off
echo 'on 0' | cec-client -s -d 1        # TV on

Not every TV supports CEC, and it may need turning on in the TV's menu (brands call it Anynet+, SimpLink, Bravia Sync and so on).

A mode button

Use your Day 7 button and LED: each press switches between two web pages, and the LED shows which mode is on. Python with gpiozero can restart the browser with a different address.

Schedule

Use a timer from Day 2 to switch the TV on at 9:00 and off at 18:00.

Done when

  • The TV turns on and off from a command
  • A button press switches the page and the LED shows the mode

Support drill

"The screen won't turn on in the morning." List five possible causes and how you'd test each one remotely.

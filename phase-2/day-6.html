LED power and wiring

Plan power for an LED installation so it runs cool, safe and with the right colours.

Why it matters

Most LED "bugs" are power problems: wrong colours at the far end, flicker, hot wires.

Learn

  • Current: a WS2812 LED can draw about 60 mA at full white (rule of thumb). 30 LEDs ≈ 1.8 A; 300 LEDs ≈ 18 A.
  • Supply: pick a 5 V supply rated well above your worst case, and don't run it at its limit.
  • Voltage drop: long strips get dimmer and yellow at the far end. Feed power in at several points ("power injection").
  • Fuses and wire size: thicker wire for more current; a fuse near the supply protects the wiring.
  • Common ground: controller and strip grounds must be connected.
  • Mains: never open or wire a mains (120 V) power supply yourself. Low voltage only.

Practice

  1. Use a multimeter to measure the 5 V at both ends of your strip at full white. Write the numbers down.
  2. Work out the current and supply size for a 2 m strip with 60 LEDs per metre.
  3. Draw a wiring diagram with supply, fuse, controller, strip and power injection.

Done when

  • You measured voltage drop on a real strip
  • Your coach checks your power calculation and diagram

Support drill

"The last half metre shows the wrong colours." Find the cause with a multimeter.

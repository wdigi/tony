Phase 2 · Day 6

LED power and wiring

Plan power for an LED installation so it runs cool, safe and with the right colours.

📝Update progress on the message board below ↓

Why it matters

Most LED "bugs" are power problems: wrong colours at the far end, flicker, hot wires.

Learn

  • Current: a WS2812 LED can draw about 60 mA at full white (rule of thumb). 30 LEDs ≈ 1.8 A; 300 LEDs ≈ 18 A.
  • Supply: pick a 5 V supply rated well above your worst case, and don't run it at its limit.
  • Voltage drop: long strips get dimmer and yellow at the far end. Feed power in at several points ("power injection").
  • Fuses and wire size: thicker wire for more current; a fuse near the supply protects the wiring.
  • Common ground: controller and strip grounds must be connected.
  • Mains: never open or wire a mains (120 V) power supply yourself. Low voltage only.

Practice

  1. Use a multimeter to measure the 5 V at both ends of your strip at full white. Write the numbers down.
  2. Work out the current and supply size for a 2 m strip with 60 LEDs per metre.
  3. Draw a wiring diagram with supply, fuse, controller, strip and power injection.

Wire and fuses

Thicker wire carries more current without getting hot. Wire size is often given in AWG: the smaller the number, the thicker the wire.

WireCareful rule of thumb for short 5 V runs
22 AWGup to about 2 A
20 AWGup to about 3 A
18 AWGup to about 5 A
16 AWGup to about 8 A
14 AWGup to about 12 A

These are deliberately careful numbers; check the rating printed on the wire. The fuse goes near the supply, rated a bit above the normal current but below what the wire can carry. Then a short circuit blows the fuse, not the wire. Your coach checks every design before it's installed.

Other things that keep LEDs healthy

  • Heat: strips at full white get hot. Mount them on aluminium channel, and limit brightness.
  • 12 V and 24 V strips (such as WS2815) carry less current for the same light, so long runs drop less voltage. The data still works the same way.
  • Controller near the strip: long data wires pick up noise. Keep the data run short; run power longer if needed.

Done when

  • You measured voltage drop on a real strip
  • Your coach checks your power calculation and diagram
  • You can size a supply with headroom and a brightness cap
  • You can explain AWG (smaller number = thicker wire)
  • You can say where the fuse goes and how it's sized

Support drill

"The last half metre shows the wrong colours." Find the cause with a multimeter.

More cases

Try each one before you open the answer.

A power wire is warm to the touch.

Too much current for that wire. Switch off. Use thicker wire, shorter runs, or lower brightness, and check the fuse is sized for the wire.

The fuse blows the moment the strip is switched on.

Switch-on surge (inrush), or a short. Check for a short with the multimeter first. If none, a capacitor at the strip input and a slow-blow fuse of the right rating help.

It worked on the bench, fails on site with a 10 m data cable.

The data wire is too long. Move the controller near the strip and run power the long distance instead.

Colours are right at 30 % brightness, wrong at 100 %.

Power, not data. Voltage drops when the current rises. Measure at the far end at 100 %; add power injection.

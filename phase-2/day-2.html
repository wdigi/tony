Phase 2 · Day 2

Scheduled jobs

Make the Pi run checks on its own, on a schedule, and keep a log.

Why it matters

Machines in a showroom or the lab run for weeks. Scheduled checks catch problems before a customer does.

Practice

Run your health script every 10 minutes with cron:

crontab -e
# add this line:
*/10 * * * * /home/yourname/bin/health.sh >> /home/yourname/health.log 2>&1

Then do the same job with a systemd timer, the more modern way. Create /etc/systemd/system/health.service and health.timer:

# health.service
[Service]
Type=oneshot
ExecStart=/home/yourname/bin/health.sh
User=yourname

# health.timer
[Timer]
OnBootSec=2min
OnUnitActiveSec=10min
[Install]
WantedBy=timers.target
sudo systemctl daemon-reload
sudo systemctl enable --now health.timer
systemctl list-timers
journalctl -u health -n 20

Done when

  • The log grows by one line every 10 minutes
  • You can explain when to use cron and when a timer

Support drill

The cron job never runs. Find out why (hint: paths and permissions inside cron are not the same as in your terminal).

Phase 4 · Day 1

3D basics

The ideas behind every 3D tool: shapes, units, axes and the two kinds of 3D.

📝Update progress on the message board below ↓

Learn

  • Axes: X left–right, Y front–back, Z up (in most CAD tools).
  • Units: DiGiKits works in millimetres at real scale. A 1 m desk is 1000 mm in the model.
  • CAD (solid) models are exact shapes for making real parts: enclosures, brackets.
  • Meshes are surfaces of tiny triangles, used for showing things on screen: showrooms, games.
  • File types: STEP (exact CAD), STL (mesh, for 3D printing), GLB/glTF (mesh with colours, for the web).

Practice

Install FreeCAD (freecad.org). Open a sample file, orbit, pan and zoom, and measure two parts.

Which way is up?

What's inside a mesh

  • Vertices: points in space. Faces: triangles joining three vertices. Normals: which way each face looks, so light knows how to shade it.
  • A curved CAD surface becomes many small flat triangles when you export a mesh. More triangles = smoother, but bigger and slower.
  • For the web and the Big Screen, fewer triangles is better. A whole showroom scene should stay in the hundreds of thousands of triangles, not millions.

The mouse style can be changed (Edit → Preferences → Display → Navigation). Pick one, stick with it. Use the Measure tool on two edges and check it against the real part with a ruler or calipers.

Real parts need room to fit

Done when

  • You can explain CAD vs mesh and when each is used
  • You can move around a model comfortably
  • You can say which way is up, and which units, in CAD and on the web
  • You can explain vertices, faces and normals
  • You measured a real part and the model, and they agree

More cases

Try each one before you open the answer.

A 100 mm part opens in another program 100 metres long.

Units mismatch: the file was read as metres (or the STL had no units). Re-import choosing millimetres, or scale by 0.001.

Your part shows up on the web lying on its side.

Z-up from CAD, Y-up on the web. Rotate it −90° around X when exporting or loading.

The round knob looks like a hexagon in the exported STL.

Mesh too coarse. Export with a finer setting (smaller deviation), but only as fine as it needs to look.
